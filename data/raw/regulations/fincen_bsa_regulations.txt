================================================================================
FINANCIAL CRIMES ENFORCEMENT NETWORK (FinCEN) - STATUTORY & REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
TITLE 31, CODE OF FEDERAL REGULATIONS (CFR) - BANK SECRECY ACT (BSA) GUIDELINES
================================================================================

1. CURRENCY TRANSACTION REPORTING (CTR) REQUIREMENTS
Reference: 31 CFR § 1010.310 / 31 CFR § 1020.311
- Mandatory Requirement: A financial institution must file a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) for each transaction in currency (deposit, withdrawal, exchange of currency, or other payment or transfer) of more than $10,000 by, through, or to the institution.
- Aggregation Rule: Multiple currency transactions must be treated as a single transaction if the institution has knowledge that they are conducted by or on behalf of any person and result in either cash in or cash out totaling more than $10,000 during any one business day.

2. ANTI-STRUCTURING PROHIBITIONS & CRIMINAL LIABILITY
Reference: 31 U.S.C. § 5324 / 31 CFR § 1010.314
- Statutory Definition: No person shall, for the purpose of evading the reporting requirements of 31 CFR § 1010.310 or § 1020.311, structure or assist in structuring, or attempt to structure or assist in structuring, any transaction with one or more domestic financial institutions.
- Elements of Structuring: Structuring occurs when a customer conducts or attempts to conduct one or more transactions in currency, in any amount, at one or more financial institutions, on one or more days, in any manner, for the purpose of evading CTR reporting.
- Typical Smurfing Pattern: Conducting multiple consecutive cash deposits just below the $10,000 reporting threshold (e.g., $9,200, $9,400, $9,850) over consecutive days or hours across one or more accounts.
- Willful Violations: Under 31 U.S.C. § 5324(d), willful violations carry severe criminal penalties and civil forfeiture of structured funds.

3. SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORTING (SAR) OBLIGATIONS FOR DEPOSITORY INSTITUTIONS
Reference: 31 CFR § 1020.320 / FinCEN BSA Form 111
- Mandatory Filing Criteria: A bank shall file a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) with FinCEN regarding any suspicious transaction relevant to a possible violation of law or regulation when:
  (a) Insider Abuse: Any known or suspected federal criminal violation committed or attempted by an insider of any dollar amount.
  (b) Aggregated Transactions of $5,000 or More With Identified Subject: Transactions conducted or attempted by, at, or through the bank where the bank knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect that:
      i. The transaction involves funds derived from illegal activities or is intended or conducted to hide or disguise funds or assets derived from illegal activities.
      ii. The transaction is designed to evade any BSA regulation, including anti-structuring provisions under 31 U.S.C. 5324.
      iii. The transaction has no business or apparent lawful purpose or is not the sort in which the particular customer would normally be expected to engage, and the bank knows of no reasonable explanation for the transaction after examining the available facts.
- Filing Timeline: The SAR must be filed no later than 30 calendar days after the date of initial detection of facts that may constitute a basis for filing. If no suspect was identified on the date of detection, the bank may delay filing up to 60 calendar days to identify a suspect.
- SAR Narrative Standard: Part V of BSA Form 111 requires a chronological, factual narrative detailing the Who, What, When, Where, Why, and How. The narrative must detail:
  - Subject identification and accounts involved
  - Exact breakdown of transaction amounts, dates, and methods
  - Red flags and lack of legitimate economic purpose
  - Nexus to potential illicit activity or sanctions evasion
  - Recommended disposition (account closure, law enforcement referral).
- Prohibition on Disclosure (Safe Harbor & Tipping-Off): Under 31 U.S.C. § 5318(g)(2), financial institutions and their personnel are strictly prohibited from notifying any person involved in the transaction that the transaction has been reported.
